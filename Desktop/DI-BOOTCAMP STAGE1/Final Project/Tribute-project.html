Mzee Jomo Kenyatta

The Founding Father of Independent Kenya (c. 1897 – 1978)

Mzee Jomo Kenyatta, anti-colonial leader and first President of Kenya.
"Our children may learn about the heroes of the past. Our task is to make ourselves the architects of the future."
- Mzee Jomo Kenyatta

Life & Legacy

  • c. 1897: Born Kamau wa Ngengi in Ngenda, Kiambu District, Kenya.
  • 1928: Became General Secretary of the Kikuyu Central Association (KCA) and began publishing the Gikuyu language newspaper Mwigwitania.
  • 1947: Elected President of the Kenya African Union (KAU), advocating for African rights and independence.
  • 1952: Arrested alongside the "Kapenguria Six" during the State of Emergency and imprisoned by British colonial authorities.
  • 1961: Released from restriction and assumed leadership of the Kenya African National Union (KANU).
  • December 12, 1963: Led Kenya to independence from British rule, serving as the first Prime Minister.
  • December 12, 1964: Sworn in as the first President of the Republic of Kenya under the banner of "Harambee" (Pulling Together).
  • August 22, 1978: Passed away in Mombasa, leaving a lasting legacy as Mzee (the Wise Old Man / Elder).

Key Pillars of His Legacy

Harambee Spirit

Championed the national motto "Harambee" (let us pull together), uniting communities for nation-building and self-reliance.

Pan-Africanism

Organized the 5th Pan-African Congress in Manchester (1945) alongside leaders like Kwame Nkrumah and W.E.B. Du Bois.

Economic Stability

Promoted a market-based economy and agricultural modernization that laid the foundation for post-independence Kenya.
